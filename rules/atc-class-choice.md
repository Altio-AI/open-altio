# One class per drug, by its use

WHO gives some drugs one class per use: timolol tablets are a heart drug, timolol eye drops a glaucoma drug. Each drug record gets the one class its dose, route or stated use points to; when the report does not say, it gets the broader class its uses share, or "can't tell". Every class it could be is kept as well.

WHO gives many substances more than one code, one per use: timolol is a heart drug as a tablet (C07AA06) and a glaucoma drug as eye drops (S01ED01); aspirin is an antithrombotic at low dose (B01AC06) and a painkiller at full dose (N02BA01). A drug record is one drug of one report (`era_T`, `primaryid`, `quarter_T`, `drug_seq`), which since 2024Q4 FDA can write on several rows, one per dosage regimen. Each record gets one class, chosen in this order:

1. **Only one code.** What the record was matched on (rule atc-index) has one WHO code. A group class (rule atc-group-classes) is always the record's one code, and says so (`group_class`).
2. **Dose.** A row of `dose_rules` names the substance or name the record was matched on, the record states at least one dose, every dose it states is in the rule's `unit` (`dose_unit`, in capitals) and within its bound (`dose_amt` at most `at_most`, or at least `at_least`), and the rule's code is one of the record's codes. A rule with a `freq` list also needs the record to state a frequency, and every frequency it states to be in the list (`dose_freq`, in capitals). A record that states no dose is not chosen by dose. Aspirin at 162 mg or less is the low-dose antithrombotic; a larger dose decides nothing, since many records at a painkiller's dose are for the heart. Finasteride 1 mg treats hair loss (D11AX10) and 5 mg the prostate (G04CB01). Methotrexate at 25 mg or less once a week is the immunosuppressant (L04AX03); taken daily, or with no frequency stated, it may be the cancer drug. Levonorgestrel's contraceptive forms (the daily pill, the implant and the hormonal IUD) need the product name or an intrauterine route to tell them apart, not a dose; only the emergency pill's 1.5 mg or less is chosen by dose.
3. **Route.** The record's route, as the route step reads it (`route_T` of `drug_route_T`, rule drug-route), is in one of the route step's drug-class groups (its `atc_group_table`), and exactly one of the record's codes fits that group. `route_groups` gives the codes each group fits. A group with `codes` fits the codes that start with one of them: eye drops fit S01 and S03. A code under `eye_and_ear_codes` (S03, WHO's class for products made for both eye and ear) fits only when no other code of the record fits the group, so eye drops take their S01 code and ear drops their S02 code. A group without `codes` fits every code except those for local use, `local_codes` (mouth, skin, eye, ear, nose, throat, lungs by inhalation, vagina, haemorrhoids, topical joint and muscle products), and its own `not_codes`: by mouth is never a general anaesthetic (N01A), and an injection never acts in the gut (A06, A07). A route in no group, `Unknown` or none says nothing, and nor does a record whose rows give different routes, which the route step reads as `Unknown`. A route group of the route step that `route_groups` does not list stops the build.
4. **Indication.** Only when neither dose nor route chose, and only among the codes they leave open: when the record's route is in a group (step 3), the codes that group fits, so a record reported on the skin is never given an eye code, and none when the group fits none of its codes; otherwise (no route, or one in no group) all its codes. A row of `indication_rules` names the substance or name the record was matched on, one code (`code`) and the indications that mean that use (`indications`), each a whole MedDRA term. An indication the report gives for this drug (`indi_pt` of `indi_latest_T`, for the record's `drug_seq`) matches a row when it is one of the row's terms, whatever its capital letters: "Rheumatoid arthritis" matches the term `rheumatoid arthritis`, and "Chest pain" does not match `pain`. When the indications that match a row whose code is one of those left open all point to one code, the record takes it (`indication`). An indication no such row matches says nothing, and indications pointing to two codes decide nothing. A row is added only once its indications are checked against the release.

    The rows give the use the reported indication means for 22 substances: methotrexate's cancer class (L01BA01) for a cancer or chemotherapy, else its immune class (L04AX03); fentanyl's anaesthetic (N01AH01) for anaesthesia, else the painkiller (N02AB03); aspirin's painkiller (N02BA01) for pain, fever or headache, else the blood thinner (B01AC06); the eye code of dexamethasone, prednisolone, bevacizumab, ciclosporin and timolol for an eye disease or eye operation, else their other code (none for timolol); tacrolimus's skin code (D11AH01) for eczema, else the transplant class; the hair codes of minoxidil and finasteride for hair loss, else their other use; ibuprofen's heart code (C01EB16) for an open heart duct in a newborn, else M01AE01; salbutamol's inhaler code (R03AC02) for inhalation; and for prednisone, methylprednisolone, sodium oxybate, celecoxib, folic acid (`vitamin b9`), diphenhydramine, naproxen, diclofenac and cetirizine the one code all their indications give. Fluticasone, a 23rd, has no row: its inhaled and its nasal use each have two codes, one per ester, and a row takes one code.
5. **The class its uses share.** Otherwise, for a record that is not a split combination (below), the record gets the deepest class at a level of `shared_class_levels` that its codes all share (`shared_class`): the class of their first 5 characters (level 4), else of their first 4 (level 3), else of their first 3 (level 2). Never level 1, which names only a body system. The codes compared are, in this order:
    - when its route is in a group and two or more of its codes fit that group (step 3), those codes: nasal fluticasone, whose nasal codes are R01AD08 and R01AD12, is a nasal corticosteroid (R01AD);
    - otherwise (no route, a route in no group, or a route none of its codes fits), all its possible codes: fluticasone with no route stays "can't tell", because its codes include skin (D07AC17) and lung (R03BA05) ones.

    Over all its codes the class holds whichever use the report meant; over the codes its route fits it carries the route's error, as a class chosen by route does. `atc_level_T` says the level.
6. **Can't tell.** Otherwise the record's class is "can't tell" (`cannot_tell`): the report does not say which use it was, and the codes step 5 compares share no class at those levels, or it is a split combination.

**Codes our table reads as substance codes.** WHO classes products, and names some codes after one substance in classes made for products of another kind. Our table, keyed by substance, takes such a code for that substance's own, so the choice reads these two kinds of class as WHO means them. This is our table's reading, not an error of WHO's.

- **Eye and ear.** S03 is for products made for both eye and ear. A substance with an S03 code has an S01 or S02 code too, which a report saying eye or ear takes first (route, above).
- **Mixes.** Some classes are for products that mix two or more substances, though WHO names each code in them after one: hydrocortisone is D07AA02 alone and D07XA01 in "corticosteroids, weak, other combinations"; phenylephrine is R01AA04 alone and R01AB01 in "sympathomimetics, combinations excl. corticosteroids". A record of one ingredient never has a code under a class of `mix_classes` among its possible codes, unless WHO's name for the code names the whole product (`norgestimate and ethinylestradiol`, G03AA11), which a record of one ingredient has only through a bridge that lets the one part it names stand for the product (rule atc-spelling-bridges). The list holds the classes of our table whose WHO name says their products are combinations (", combinations", "in combination", "combinations with"), except those that say they exclude or include combinations or hold plain products (A11C, A11D, A11G, G01A, R05C, R05D) and those named "Combinations of" or "Combinations and", whose codes name every ingredient or are one substance (sultamicillin, J01CR04; the antacid complexes of A02AD). A class in the list holds every code below it. A combination keeps these codes.

A combination with no WHO combination code is split into its ingredients, each with its codes, whether the substance dictionary or FDA's active ingredient named it (`each_ingredient`, `each_active_ingredient`). Its class is always "can't tell", even when its ingredients' codes share a class: WHO classes a combination as a whole, so neither one ingredient's code nor the class its ingredients share need be the product's. Calcium's and vitamin D's combination is A12AX, not vitamin D's A11CC. Every possible code of every record, whatever was chosen, is kept in `atc_drug_codes_T`.

Counts of classes are of cases (rule atc-index).

**Why:** a fixed first code per substance, as DiAna's ATC file gives, files every timolol eye drop under heart drugs and most aspirin under painkillers, although 88% of aspirin records that state a dose in mg are 162 mg or less. Route and dose are what the report says about the use, so they decide where they can, and the indication it gives for the drug decides after them, only among the codes they leave open, so it never overrules them or gives a class the route rules out (diclofenac gel taken for arthritis is not the tablet, nor prednisolone tablets taken for an eye disease the eye drops); an indication is read as a whole term, since a word inside a term can name another use (chest pain is not pain). The indication rows are a reviewed list of the indications reported for these substances, read under those uses; where dose or route also decide, the indications point to the code they chose for 99.4% of records. Where none of the three decides, the class all the uses share is still certain, and only below it is "can't tell" said rather than guessed. FAERS routes are themselves wrong a few times in a hundred (about 3% of latanoprost eye drops are reported as topical), so a class chosen by route carries that error. Reading S03 and the classes for mixes as WHO means them adds no wrong class; it takes away possible codes that kept a route from deciding. Each dose bound agrees with the indication the report gives for the drug in at least 95% of the records it decides that name one use, except finasteride at 5 mg (89%). No bound tells levonorgestrel's contraceptive forms apart: most records at 13.5 mg or more are implants (Jadelle, Norplant), not the IUD, and the IUD Kyleena is reported at 17.5 µg a day, inside a pill's bound. The groups and bounds are those the release's own records support (`notebooks/faers/09_drug_classes`).
